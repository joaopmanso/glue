GLUE listens to every file and judges what it really is, not what its name or format says.

## The labels
- **Lossless**: a real lossless file (CD quality or better).
- **Genuine hi-res**: really more than CD quality, with music above 22 kHz.
- **Lossy**: an MP3, AAC or similar, as its format says.
- **Transcode**: a lossless file made from a lossy one (the lossy cut-off shows in its spectrum).
- **Upsampled**: a "hi-res" file with nothing above CD quality.
- **Padded**: "24-bit" that is really 16-bit.
- **Caution**: something to look at; the song's page says what.

## Lower quality
The **Lower quality** view lists songs below a lossless file of their format. It's for information: nothing has to be done. If you know a song is fine, **Not a problem** on its page takes it off the list.

## The analysis
GLUE analyses in the background, several songs at a time (the bar at the top of the library). It also finds BPM, key and a fingerprint for duplicates. **Background analysis** turns it off; songs you open are still analysed.
